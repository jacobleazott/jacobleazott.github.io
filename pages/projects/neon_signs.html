Neon Signs

Overview

You have most certainly come across custom LED neon signs before. At least when I first began creating these it seemed most were simply attached to a piece of acrylic, acceptable from a distance but showed wiring and plastic housing up close. Why not fully 3D print the enclosure? It would provide a cleaner look and be far more durable. Over time I developed a streamlined process to go from a blank canvas to a fully finished sign in under a day (depending on size and complexity of course). I have since made frankly way too many signs, not only for myself but for friends and family.

How to Create Your Own Neon Sign

This step-by-step guide will walk you through building your own customizable Langton's Ant simulator.

Step 0: Tools and Materials

There are a few different ways to create your own sign and there is plenty of room for differing tools and materials depending on what is available to you. However, the following are what I use.

Tools

  • 3D Modeling Software
  • Vector Graphics Software
  • 3D Printer
  • Soldering Iron
  • Wire Strippers
  • Hot Glue Gun (optional)

Materials

  • LED 'Neon' Rope (color(s) of your choice)
  • 3D Printer Filament (I use PLA)
  • 12V DC Power Supply
  • DC Barrel Plugs or Cables
  • Wire
  • Solder
  • Hot Glue (optional)
  • Liquid Electrical Tape (optional)

Step 1: Design

Usually the most difficult part of this process is coming up with a design that you like and that is reasonable. The decisions you make in this step will determine the time needed and overall complexity of wiring your sign together.

In your vector graphics software of choice draw out your sign treating single lines as segments of the sign. Set your stroke width for that line to 9.5mm. This allows for 2mm walls on either side of the 5.5mm wide LED 'rope'. If you are using different sized LEDs or want thinner/ thicker walls you should make that decision now.

An important note here is to make sure any curves or corners have at least a 3mm radius so that the LED rope can bend correctly.

Now most LED neon rope is cuttable in 25mm segments. This is crucial in the design step as you will want to make sure your vector segments are close to that size. Now this is where you will need to simply have some experience to know what are acceptable tolerances for the lengths. There are many ways to fix this in the wiring stage but from personal experience I have found that shooting for around +/- 10-15 mm outside of the 25mm segments is usually good enough. This is because depending on curves and if you lay the LEDs on the inner or outer radius you will have differeing lengths. You also need to make sure to have room for having a soldered connection to the end of each segment. For purely straight segments you can get a lot closer to the 25mm lengh but make sure you are always overshooting a bit. It is a lot easier to put the LED segments into 30mm segments than 24mm segments.

In Adobe Illustrator it is very easy to get this information. Just open the 'Document Info' tab, select 'Objects', then select your singular path. It will display the length under 'Paths'. If you are too close to a 25mm increment consider shortening/ lengthening the segment or playing around with the curves.

Once you have your design finallized it is time to export it as an SVG for our 3D modeling software.
